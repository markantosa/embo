EMBO TECHNICAL ADVISORY
Ultrasound Sizing (UAS) and Computer Vision (CV) Subsystems — Necessary Changes
Date: July 2026
Status: Testing phase, pre-exhibition

=====================================================================
SUMMARY
=====================================================================
Testing has surfaced two independent problems: (1) single-frequency
ultrasound attenuation cannot reliably resolve a heterogeneous/polydisperse
particle population, and (2) the current CV imaging setup cannot see
particles clearly and cannot separate overlapping ones. Neither issue
invalidates the core device concept. Both are known, well-characterized
problems in particle measurement with established fixes. This memo lists
the changes needed, in priority order.

=====================================================================
1. ULTRASOUND (UAS) SIZING
=====================================================================

PROBLEM
A single 1MHz attenuation reading is not uniquely invertible into a
particle size when the population is heterogeneous. Different size
distributions can produce the same attenuation number. Attenuation is
also highly size-sensitive (Rayleigh-regime scattering scales roughly
as d^6), so a small number of large/clumped particles can dominate the
bulk signal even when the median particle is much smaller. This is a
known limitation in the ultrasonic particle-sizing literature, which is
why industry moved toward multi-frequency attenuation spectroscopy
rather than single-frequency readings.

REQUIRED CHANGES
- Do not treat the current single-frequency attenuation signal as an
  independent, trustworthy size measurement. Re-scope its role.
- Empirically validate before trusting it: at several stroke counts,
  simultaneously log (a) CV-measured true median + IQR and (b) raw
  1MHz attenuation. Plot correlation. If it is a clean, monotonic
  relationship across your real operating range, it may be usable as
  a coarse/fast proxy. If it is weak or non-monotonic, do not use it
  as a control input.
- Leverage existing hardware: the AD9833 DDS chip is frequency-agile.
  No board respin is needed to test 2-3 discrete frequencies instead
  of one — this is a firmware/timing change only. A small multi-point
  spectrum gives materially more information than a single point.
- Do NOT attempt full attenuation-spectroscopy inversion (ECAH model
  fitting, etc.) — this is a research-grade undertaking and out of
  scope for the remaining timeline.
- Demote ultrasound to a SECONDARY signal. The CV/camera pipeline
  remains the authoritative source for the stop-mixing decision, since
  it measures individual particles directly rather than inferring size
  from a bulk proxy. Use ultrasound as a fast continuous trend signal
  between camera reads, fused with StallGuard viscosity data, not as
  the sole PID input.

=====================================================================
2. COMPUTER VISION (CV) SIZING
=====================================================================

PROBLEM A — Particles are nearly invisible under current lighting
Hydrated gelatin/collagen particles are ~80-99% water by volume, so
their refractive index is very close to the surrounding saline
(near index-matched). Under plain backlighting this produces almost
no contrast. What the current camera actually resolves clearly is air
bubbles (large air-water refractive index mismatch = strong signal)
and glare from the cylindrical syringe wall — not the particles
themselves.

REQUIRED CHANGES
- Add a visible dye to the saline (methylene blue or similar) to give
  particles bulk optical contrast against the fluid. Test this first —
  cheapest, fastest fix, no hardware change.
- Trial dark-field illumination (angle the LED panel obliquely instead
  of straight backlight) so only light scattered by particle surfaces
  reaches the camera, suppressing the near-invisible bulk-transmission
  problem.
- Trial cross-polarization: polarizing film on the LED panel, a second
  crossed polarizer on the camera lens. This suppresses specular glare
  and bubble highlights. Note: this will strongly benefit Lyostypt
  (intact collagen, structurally birefringent) but is not expected to
  reveal much extra structure in true denatured gelatin sponge, which
  loses birefringence on denaturation — treat it as a glare-suppression
  tool for gelatin, not a contrast-generation tool.
- Address the cylindrical-barrel lensing/glare artifact separately —
  consider a flat-walled imaging window rather than imaging straight
  through the round syringe barrel.

PROBLEM B — Overlapping/stacked particles cannot be segmented
Particles overlapping or stacked in 3D within the syringe barrel are
not reliably separable from a single 2D image, regardless of
segmentation algorithm quality.

REQUIRED CHANGES
- Move the imaging site to the stopcock aperture to physically
  constrain particles closer to a single layer as they transit the
  narrow bore (same principle used in commercial flow-imaging particle
  analyzers). This requires:
    * A clear optical window at the stopcock (standard stopcocks are
      opaque plastic) — custom clear insert or modified bore.
    * Strobed, short-duration illumination synced to the camera
      trigger. Flow velocity through the narrow bore will be
      significantly higher than in the barrel; continuous lighting
      will produce motion blur even with the global-shutter sensor.
    * Early check for increased bubble/cavitation formation at the
      constriction (pressure drop can nucleate bubbles) — this location
      is also the point of maximum shear, so bubble noise may be worse
      here, not better. Verify before committing the full optical build.
- Switch the CV model from bounding-box detection to instance
  segmentation (YOLOv8-seg, or evaluate Cellpose, which is purpose-built
  for dense/touching irregular blobs). Bounding boxes cannot resolve
  overlap; per-pixel instance masks can.

PROBLEM C — Sizing metric assumes particles are spherical
Fragmented gelatin/collagen particles are irregular ("snowflake"-like),
not circular, so a simple diameter measurement is not well-defined.

REQUIRED CHANGES
- Adopt Equivalent Circular Diameter (ECD) as the primary size metric —
  the diameter of a circle with the same projected area as the particle,
  per ISO 13322 / ISO 9276-6. This is the standard used across
  commercial particle-image-analysis instruments and does not require
  particles to be round.
- Report ECD alongside max/min Feret diameter (caliper-style measurement
  across the particle at multiple angles) and solidity (particle area /
  convex hull area). The Feret spread and solidity give a second,
  independent signal for "how irregular/branching" the population still
  is — useful beyond ECD alone for tracking mixing progress.

=====================================================================
3. MATERIALS / PROCESS NOTES
=====================================================================
- Lyostypt is bovine collagen, not gelatin — structurally different
  material, explains its non-gelling stringy behavior in earlier tests.
  Not a valid stand-in for Gelfoam-equivalent testing.
- Source verified pharmaceutical-grade absorbable gelatin sponge
  (e.g. Surgispon) or Chinese-pharmacopoeia-grade 吸收性明胶海绵 for
  valid ground-truth testing material.
- A DIY gelatin foam (foamed gelatin solution, freeze-dried, then
  dehydrothermally cross-linked at 100-160C for 24-72h in a standard
  oven, no chemical crosslinker needed) is a viable cheap substitute for
  day-to-day iteration, but should be cross-validated against real
  purchased gelatin sponge before trusting absolute size calibration —
  cross-link density will affect fragmentation kinetics.

4. SUGGESTED SEQUENCING (given limited time before exhibition)
=====================================================================
1. Dye test on current setup (quick, no hardware change)
2. Empirical US-vs-CV correlation check (quick, no hardware change)
3. Dark-field / cross-polarization lighting trial
4. Switch CV model to instance segmentation
5. Stopcock optical window + strobed illumination (highest effort —
   scope this early if pursuing it, given custom part fabrication)
